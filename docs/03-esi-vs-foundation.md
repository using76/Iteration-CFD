# ESI OpenFOAM v2606 vs OpenFOAM Foundation 12

> 어느 배포판을 GPU 포팅의 기반으로 삼을지 정하기 위한, 두 트리 고유 컴포넌트의 전수 비교.

This document is for a CFD engineer choosing a base tree for a GPU port. It covers four areas: turbulence,
solvers and solver modules, discretisation and linear algebra, and the physics libraries (thermophysical,
combustion, radiation, Lagrangian, region models, atmospheric, overset, waves, engine, fvOptions/faOptions,
dynamic mesh, meshing).

Tier legend used in every ESI-only table below:

| Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A-trivial | Elementwise or bounded-stencil arithmetic. One CUDA kernel, no host round-trip. |
| B-sparse-solve | Standard assembly plus one sparse linear solve (AMGX / cuDSS / cuSPARSE). |
| C-fft | Dominated by spectral transforms (cuFFT). |
| D-hard | Search, topology mutation, ray tracing, particle tracking or dynamic allocation. |
| E-cpu-only | Runtime selection, dictionary IO or template plumbing. Resolve once at setup. |

## 1. How the Two Trees Are Laid Out

> 두 트리는 같은 라이브러리를 이름만 바꾸고 뿌리를 옮긴 것에 가깝지만, 솔버 계층 구조만은 근본적으로 다르다.

Most single-tree names are **renames, not additions**. Four structural divergences matter to a porter.

### 1.1 Turbulence: `TurbulenceModels` vs `MomentumTransportModels`

ESI's `src/TurbulenceModels` is Foundation's `src/MomentumTransportModels`. The rename runs all the way down:
`turbulenceModels/` to `momentumTransportModels/`, `turbulenceModel` to `momentumTransportModel`,
`incompressibleTurbulenceModel` to `incompressibleMomentumTransportModel`, `compressibleTurbulenceModel` to
`compressibleMomentumTransportModel`, `PhaseCompressibleTurbulenceModel` to
`phaseCompressibleMomentumTransportModel`, `PhaseIncompressibleTurbulenceModel` to
`phaseIncompressibleMomentumTransportModel`, `turbulentTransportModels.C` to
`incompressibleMomentumTransportModels.C`, `turbulentFluidThermoModels.C` to
`compressibleMomentumTransportModels.C`, `makeTurbulenceModel.H` to `makeMomentumTransportModel.H`,
`libturbulenceModels` to `libmomentumTransportModels`. There is also a pure spelling rename: ESI
`laminar/generalizedNewtonian` (z) vs Foundation `laminar/generalisedNewtonian` (s), with identical concrete laws
(BirdCarreau, Casson, CrossPowerLaw, HerschelBulkley, powerLaw, strainRateFunction) in both.

The real difference is the **instantiation machinery**. ESI has one four-parameter class template
`TurbulenceModel<Alpha, Rho, BasicTurbulenceModel, TransportModel>` plus one `makeTurbulenceModel.H` macro, giving
exactly **one** interception point for substituting device-resident model implementations. Foundation deleted that
layer and spreads the same role across `momentumTransportModel` plus four flavour bases plus four
`*MomentumTransportModelTemplates.C` files, giving **four**.

Two further ESI-side wins: `Base/SpalartAllmaras/SpalartAllmarasBase` means the SA algebra (chi, fv1, fv2, Stilda)
is written once instead of duplicated in each SA derivative, and `wallFunctionBlenders` +
`wallFunctionCoefficients` funnel every wall function's blending through one enum, which becomes one templated
device functor rather than a per-face branch. Note `Base/kOmegaSST/kOmegaSSTBase` exists in **both** trees.

DES placement is a rename, not an addition: ESI has a `turbulenceModels/DES/` directory, Foundation keeps
`SpalartAllmarasDES`, `SpalartAllmarasDDES`, `SpalartAllmarasIDDES` and `kOmegaSSTDES` under `LES/`. Those four
models exist in both trees. Likewise alphat/thermal transport is a *move*, not an ESI addition: ESI's
`compressible/EddyDiffusivity` and `alphatJayatillekeWallFunction` both exist in Foundation under
`src/ThermophysicalTransportModels/...`.

**Easier porting target for this area: ESI.**

### 1.2 Sources: `fvModels`/`fvConstraints` vs `fvOptions`

ESI's single `src/fvOptions` splits in Foundation into `src/fvModels` (sources) and `src/fvConstraints`
(constraints). Confirmed rename pairs include `explicitPorositySource` to `porosityForce`,
`interRegionExplicitPorositySource` to `interRegionPorosityForce`, `actuationDiskSource` to `actuationDisk`,
`radialActuationDiskSource` to `radialActuationDisk`, `rotorDiskSource` to `rotorDisk`,
`solidificationMeltingSource` to `solidificationMelting`, `viscousDissipation` to `viscousHeating`,
`phaseLimitStabilization` to `phaseLimitStabilisation`, `codedSource` to `codedFvModel`,
`tabulatedAccelerationSource` to `sixDoFAcceleration`/`acceleration`, `heatExchangerSource` (effectivenessTable) to
`effectivenessHeatExchanger`, `fixedTemperatureConstraint` to `fixedTemperature`, `fixedValueConstraint` to
`fixedValue`, and `limitVelocity` to `limitMag`.

Foundation additionally introduces the whole `fvFieldSource` concept, the mechanism by which `fvModels` set values
in source cells rather than only at real patches. **ESI has no `fvFieldSource` anywhere.** Foundation's
mass/heat/volume source set (`massSource`, `heatSource`, `volumeSource`, `massTransfer`, `phaseChange`) injects
into the continuity equation consistently; ESI's nearest equivalent, `semiImplicitSource`, does not touch
continuity.

**Easier porting target for this area: mixed.** Foundation's design is cleaner and its sources are pluggable;
ESI's catalogue (the whole `atm*` source family, `jouleHeatingSource`, `acousticDampingSource`,
`multiphaseStabilizedTurbulence`) is far larger and almost entirely tier A.

### 1.3 Mesh motion: `fvMeshTopoChangers`/`fvMeshMovers` vs `dynamicFvMesh`

ESI's `src/dynamicFvMesh` becomes four Foundation libraries: `fvMeshMovers`, `fvMeshTopoChangers`,
`fvMeshStitchers`, `fvMeshDistributors`. `dynamicMotionSolverFvMesh` becomes `fvMeshMovers/motionSolver`,
`dynamicInkJetFvMesh` becomes `fvMeshMovers/inkJet`, `dynamicRefineFvMesh` becomes `fvMeshTopoChangers/refiner`,
and `staticFvMesh` becomes simply "no mover". ESI's `dynamicMesh/motionSolvers` becomes Foundation's
`src/motionSolvers`; ESI's `polyTopoChange` + `meshCut` + `fvMeshSubset` + `fvMeshDistribute` become
`src/polyTopoChange`; ESI's `dynamicMesh/motionSmoother` moves to `src/mesh/snappyHexMesh/motionSmoother`.

This split is exactly the boundary a device-resident time loop needs: **points move** (tier B, portable) is cleanly
separated from **connectivity changes** (tier D, host-only). ESI's `polyTopoChanger`-modifier design interleaves
the two, and Foundation deleted the modifier framework outright.

The deepest divergence here is non-matching interfaces. ESI uses AMI/ACMI (`cyclicAMI`, `cyclicACMI`,
`dynamicMotionSolverFvMeshAMI`, `slidingInterface`) plus a full overset implementation. **Foundation-12 deleted AMI
entirely**, replacing it with `nonConformalCoupling` (`nonConformalCyclic` / `nonConformalError` /
`nonConformalProcessorCyclic` patches plus `fvMeshStitchers`), and has no overset at all. Foundation's coupling is
expressed as extra faces in the ordinary `fvMesh` addressing, so the matrix stays a plain sparse operator that
cuSPARSE/AMGX can consume. ESI's AMI and overset both require intercepting `lduMatrix` assembly and re-running
geometric searches inside the time loop.

**Easier porting target for this area: Foundation, decisively.**

### 1.4 Solvers: `foamRun` modules vs standalone executables

ESI keeps the classic layout, `applications/solvers/<physics-group>/<solverName>`, with variants nested one level
deeper as their own executables (for example `incompressible/pimpleFoam/{SRFPimpleFoam, overPimpleDyMFoam}`).
There is no `applications/modules` and no `applications/legacy` in ESI. The result is **121 buildable targets**
under `applications/solvers`, most of them near-duplicate `main()` files sharing `.H` includes by copy.

Foundation-12 restructured into three directories:

- `applications/modules/` — **24 solver modules**, each a shared library exporting a class derived from
  `Foam::solver`, plus its private sub-libraries. These are not executables.
- `applications/solvers/` — only **five** executables: `foamRun`, `foamMultiRun`, plus `boundaryFoam`, `chemFoam`
  and `potentialFoam`, which resisted modularisation.
- `applications/legacy/` — **14** unmodularised classic solvers, kept working but frozen. `laplacianFoam`,
  `financialFoam`, `PDRFoam`, `electrostaticFoam`, `magneticFoam`, `mhdFoam`, `icoFoam`, `porousSimpleFoam`,
  `rhoPorousSimpleFoam`, `shallowWaterFoam`, `dsmcFoam`, `mdFoam` and `mdEquilibrationFoam` all live here, which is
  easy to miss if you only list `applications/solvers`.

The correct mental model: one ESI executable is roughly one Foundation module name passed to `foamRun`. ESI's
`*DyM` / steady / transient variants collapse into runtime options, because every Foundation module supports mesh
motion, topology change and steady/transient PIMPLE natively. ESI's four Euler-Euler solvers
(`twoPhaseEulerFoam`, `reactingTwoPhaseEulerFoam`, `multiphaseEulerFoam`, `reactingMultiphaseEulerFoam`, backed by
five parallel libraries under `src/phaseSystemModels`) collapse into the single Foundation module
`applications/modules/multiphaseEuler`.

**Easier porting target for this area: Foundation, decisively.** A GPU port needs exactly one device-resident time
loop. `Foam::solver` already defines `preSolve` / `moveMesh` / `momentumPredictor` / `thermophysicalPredictor` /
`pressureCorrector` / `postCorrector` as virtual methods, so a GPU backend is introduced by overriding those hooks
in a handful of base classes (`fluidSolver`, `isothermalFluid`, `VoFSolver`) and every derived module inherits it.
ESI's layout would force you to touch ~120 near-duplicate `main()` files that each re-inline the same PIMPLE loop
via `.H` includes, with no shared class to hook.

### 1.5 Summary of the layout verdict

| Sub-area | Easier target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turbulence models | **ESI** | One template + one macro = one interception point; shared SA/SST bases; `wallFunctionBlenders`. |
| Discretisation / linear algebra | **ESI** | `lowerCSRAddr`/`lowerCSR` CSR bridge already exists; `solveScalar` mixed precision; `src/fused`. |
| Sources (fvModels vs fvOptions) | Mixed | Foundation's design is cleaner; ESI's catalogue is far larger and mostly tier A. |
| Mesh motion and topology | **Foundation** | Mover/topoChanger/stitcher split matches the device-resident boundary; nonConformalCoupling beats AMI. |
| Solver structure | **Foundation** | One `Foam::solver` virtual interface vs ~120 duplicated `main()` files. |

### 2.3 Porting order implied by the tiers

The tier-A elementwise work (sigma/Ssigma, SpalartAllmarasBase algebra, the three deltas, the `atm*` and blended
wall functions, DEShybrid weights) is self-contained and can land first. Every tier-B entry bottoms out in the same
convection-diffusion assembly plus one sparse solve, so a single AMGX/cuDSS-backed `fvScalarMatrix` path unlocks
EBRSM, GEKO, kEpsilonPhitF, both SST hybrids, kL and kEpsilonLopesdaCosta at once — with EBRSM's
`fvSymmTensorMatrix` driven as a six-RHS multi-vector solve against one shared matrix.

The real blocker for a fully device-resident time loop in this area is not any model: it is **wallDist** (a
FaceCellWave/meshWave search, tier D), needed by SpalartAllmaras*, kOmegaSST*, EBRSM, SLADelta, IDDESDelta,
vanDriestDelta and DEShybrid. For a static mesh it is computed once, so a host-side wallDist plus a single upload
is sufficient and no model above needs to be downgraded; for a moving or topo-changing mesh it must be solved on
device or it reintroduces a per-step round-trip. Two smaller ones in the same category: `smoothDelta` (also
FaceCellWave, present in both trees) and the ESI `schemes/DEShybrid` dependency, which forces the
`surfaceInterpolationScheme` abstraction — not just turbulence — to be GPU-aware.

**Costs of choosing ESI here:** re-adding v2f (plus its two wall functions), kOmega2006, the Giesekus / PTT /
lambdaThixotropic viscoelastic set, and accepting no `fvFieldSource` layer.

### 3.4 Suggested bring-up order

`scalarTransportFoam` and `surfactantFoam` (B, single field, fixed matrix), then `acousticFoam` / `thermoFoam` /
`solidEquilibriumDisplacementFoam` (B, matrix assembled once so a single cuDSS factorisation serves the whole run),
then `dnsFoam` (C, the only place cuFFT is the whole point), then the SRF and Boussinesq pairs (B, elementwise
additions to an existing PIMPLE), then the VOF group (B once a device MULES limiter exists), and defer everything
Lagrangian, overset-with-motion, isoAdvector and adjoint-outer-loop (D) until last. Runtime selection of thermo,
viscosity, turbulence and radiation models is E-cpu-only everywhere in both trees: resolve every selection ONCE at
setup into a fixed kernel rather than dispatching virtual calls per cell inside the loop, or the
no-host-round-trip requirement cannot be met.

## 4. Discretisation and Linear Algebra

**Checkout artifact, read first.** Both trees are checked out on a case-insensitive Windows filesystem, so
upstream's two sibling directories `src/OpenFOAM/matrices/lduMatrix` (the scalar, run-time-selectable matrix) and

that lists as `LduMatrix`, and likewise their `solvers`/`Solvers`, `preconditioners`/`Preconditioners`,
`smoothers`/`Smoothers` subdirectories. Paths below are the on-disk merged paths; canonical upstream paths for the
scalar solvers are `src/OpenFOAM/matrices/lduMatrix/solvers/{FPCG,PPCG,PPCR}` (confirmed by reading the headers:
all three derive from `lduMatrix::solver`, not from `LduMatrix<...>::solver`). The merge is identical in both

fvm, and fvcSmooth. Most importantly, **GAMG is file-for-file identical** in both trees — same 4 agglomerations
(pair, algebraicPair, faceAreaPair, dummy), same 6 proc-agglomerations, same interfaces/interfaceFields, same
`GAMGSolver{,AgglomerateMatrix,Interpolate,Scale,Solve}`. **fvAgglomerationMethods is also identical** (MGridGen +
pairPatchAgglomeration, same 11 files). Preconditioners (6) and smoothers (8, including DILUGaussSeidel) match
exactly. The multigrid and preconditioner layer therefore gives no reason to prefer one tree.

Other renames that are not additions: ESI `deferredCorrection` == Foundation `deferred`; ESI `schemesLookup` vs
Foundation's self-contained `fvSchemes.C`; `cellPointWeight` differs only in nesting depth (but
`cellPointWeightWallModified` is genuinely ESI-only, Foundation folding that logic into
`interpolationCellPointWallModified.C`); Foundation's `algorithms/FvFaceCellWave` vs ESI's
`meshTools/algorithms/MeshWave/FaceCellWave`. And note `skewCorrected` exists as a surface-interpolation scheme in
BOTH trees — only `skewCorrectedSnGrad` is ESI-only. Do not conflate them.

### 4.4 The finiteArea asymmetry

Foundation-12 has no `src/finiteArea` directory at all — the entire finite-area stack (faMesh, faMatrix, fa
schemes, edge interpolation, volSurfaceMapping, FA fields and BCs) is ESI-only, along with its dependent libraries
`src/dynamicFaMesh`, `src/faOptions` and `src/regionFaModels`. This is the largest single block of ESI-only code in
this area, about 20 of the components listed. **Porting note:** an faMesh is an LDU graph exactly like an fvMesh
(edge owner/neighbour instead of face owner/neighbour), and faMatrix derives directly from lduMatrix. So if you
write your GPU discretisation kernels and CSR conversion generically over an LDU graph rather than hardwiring
fvMesh, the whole FA stack comes along nearly free — and conversely, if you hardwire fvMesh now you will pay for it
twice. Adopt ESI's own factoring here: it is not an accident that ESI pulled `schemesLookup` and `solution` into
the shared core so both fv and fa could derive from them.

## 6. Which Tree to Port From

> 결론: 코드는 ESI v2606에서 가져오고, 솔버 구조는 Foundation-12의 모듈 설계를 따라라.

### 6.1 The recommendation

**Base the port on ESI OpenFOAM v2606, but build the device time loop against a Foundation-style solver-module
API that you introduce into the ESI tree.**

The four area reviews do not point the same way, and that is the whole answer. Three of the four say ESI; one — the
one about how a solver is *structured* — says Foundation, and it says so for the single most load-bearing
requirement in a GPU port: **there must be exactly one device-resident time loop.**

The resolution is that ESI's advantages are in code you would otherwise have to write, while Foundation's advantage
is in an interface you can write in an afternoon.

### 6.2 Why ESI for the code

**Linear algebra — the decisive one.** ESI's `lduAddressing` already carries `lowerCSRAddr()`/`calcLoCSR()`,
`lduMatrix` caches `lowerCSR()`, and `lduAddressing::map<Type>()` permutes any face array into CSR order via
`losortStartAddr`/`losortAddr`. That is the entire LDU-to-cuSPARSE/AMGX/cuDSS bridge, already written and
demand-driven-cached. Foundation-12 has none of it. Alongside that: the whole ESI solver/smoother/preconditioner
API is expressed on `solveScalarField`, separating storage precision from solve precision (Foundation has zero
occurrences of `solveScalar`) — on a 16 GB card, fp32 storage with fp64 reductions is the difference between
fitting a case and not. And `src/fused` (libfusedFiniteVolume) plus the `fvMatrixExpression` prototype are
upstream's own work on eliminating temporaries and fusing operator evaluation, which is precisely the
restructuring a GPU port has to do anyway. Secondary but real: FPCG/PPCG/PPCR (pipelined Krylov, one reduction per
iteration — the GPU-friendly form), `fvGeometryScheme` (metric computation as a pluggable strategy),
`zoltanRenumber`/`noRenumber` (ordering control, which sets SpMV coalescing), and
`lduPrimitiveMeshAssembly`/`lduCalculatedProcessorField` (proof that an lduMatrix can be assembled and solved with
no fvMesh attached).

Note that GAMG, `fvAgglomerationMethods`, the 6 preconditioners, the 8 smoothers, the 24 limitedSchemes and the 12
multivariateSchemes are file-for-file identical in both trees, so the multigrid layer gives no reason either way.

**Turbulence.** One `TurbulenceModel<Alpha,Rho,Basic,Transport>` template plus one `makeTurbulenceModel.H` macro
gives exactly one interception point for substituting device-resident model implementations; Foundation's four
parallel `make*MomentumTransportModel.H` headers mean four. `SpalartAllmarasBase` and `kOmegaSSTBase` mean each
family's elementwise math is written once, so it becomes one CUDA kernel per family rather than one per derived
model. `wallFunctionBlenders`/`wallFunctionCoefficients` turn all wall-function blending into one enum-selected
functor resolvable at kernel-template-instantiation time — the difference between a divergence-free boundary
kernel and a per-face branch.

**Physics breadth.** Overset, finite-area (and with it faOptions, regionFaModels, dynamicFaMesh), engine, surface
film, pyrolysis, thermal baffles, solid chemistry, SLGThermo, barotropicCompressibilityModel, isoAdvector, SRF,
coalCloud, Kmesh/UOprocess, laser/DTRM, adjoint optimisation and the whole atmospheric source-term set exist
**only** in ESI. Radiation is a strict ESI superset. Those are not features you re-add cheaply.

**The finiteArea multiplier.** An faMesh is an LDU graph exactly like an fvMesh (edge owner/neighbour instead of
face owner/neighbour), and `faMatrix` derives directly from `lduMatrix`. If you write the GPU discretisation
kernels and CSR conversion generically over an LDU graph rather than hardwiring fvMesh, the entire FA stack —
about 20 components, plus liquidFilmFoam, surfactantFoam, sphereSurfactantFoam, compressibleInterFilmFoam and
kinematicParcelFoam — comes along nearly free. Hardwire fvMesh now and you pay for it twice.

### 6.3 What you must import from Foundation

Foundation's contribution is architectural, not code you copy:

1. **A `Foam::solver`-shaped module interface.** Define `preSolve` / `moveMesh` / `momentumPredictor` /
   `thermophysicalPredictor` / `pressureCorrector` / `postCorrector` as virtual hooks and put the GPU backend
   behind them in a handful of base classes. Without this you are patching ~120 near-duplicate ESI `main()` files
   that each re-inline the same PIMPLE loop through `.H` includes, with no shared class to hook.
2. **The mover / topoChanger / stitcher / distributor separation** for mesh motion. Keep "points move" (tier B,
   portable) strictly apart from "connectivity changes" (tier D, host-only). Do not carry ESI's
   `polyTopoChanger`-modifier design into the port; treat `topoChangerFvMesh`, `slidingInterface`,
   `layerAdditionRemoval` and `attachDetach` as out of scope or frozen-topology-only.
3. **`nonConformalCoupling` as the model for non-matching interfaces**, wherever you can choose. Foundation
   expresses the coupling as extra faces in ordinary `fvMesh` addressing, so the matrix stays a plain sparse
   operator that cuSPARSE/AMGX can consume. ESI's AMI/ACMI requires intercepting `lduMatrix` assembly and
   re-running geometric searches inside the time loop. Where you need ESI overset, export an explicit CSR (with
   interpolation rows substituted) and hand it to AMGX rather than reproducing ESI's lduMatrix trickery —
   `fvMeshPrimitiveLduAddressing` is the explicit-addressing form that export needs.
4. **The non-templated cloud interface.** Foundation's `parcelCloud` + `clouds` fvModel decouple the cloud from
   the solver's template instantiation. ESI's four-library, deeply-templated Lagrangian hierarchy multiplies
   kernel instantiations enormously. Take ESI's ~25 extra submodels and cloud function objects, but hang them on
   a Foundation-shaped interface.
5. **A non-templated `combustionModel` base**, for the same reason: ESI's `CombustionModel` /
   `ChemistryCombustion` / `ThermoCombustion` template explosion produces one symbol per thermo type.

### 6.4 The bill for choosing ESI

Things you give up, and must re-add if you need them:

| Given up | Where it hurts |
| --- | --- |
| v2f + v2WallFunction + fWallFunction, kOmega2006 | Turbulence model coverage. kEpsilonPhitF is a different model, not a rename. |
| Giesekus, PTT, lambdaThixotropic | Viscoelastic / non-Newtonian coverage. |
| `fvFieldSource` layer | No way to supply BC-style values to fvModel source cells. Affects the whole source framework. |
| ThermophysicalTransportModels as a library (MaxwellStefan, Fickian multicomponent diffusion) | Species transport closures. |
| Volumetric wave forcing/damping fvModels and wave spectra | Wave cases; and these are tier-A elementwise kernels, i.e. the GPU-friendlier design. |
| `fvMeshMovers/interpolator`, `multiValveEngine`, `fvMeshStitchers`, `fvMeshDistributors` | Motion without a solve, topology-free engine motion, in-loop load balancing. |
| `meshCheck` library, `specieTransfer`, `saturationModels` at top level | Convenience and reuse, not capability. |

None of these blocks a device-resident time loop. Every item on the ESI-only side of the ledger that you would
otherwise have to write — CSR addressing, mixed precision, fused operators, the FA stack, overset, the engine and
film libraries — does.

### 6.5 Concrete bring-up plan

1. **Foundation-shape the ESI tree.** Introduce the `Foam::solver` virtual interface and a `fluidSolver` /
   `isothermalFluid` / `VoFSolver` base trio inside ESI, and re-express a handful of ESI solvers as modules
   behind it. This is the one piece of Foundation you actually port over.
2. **Build the field/matrix layer on `lowerCSRAddr` + `solveScalar`**, generically over an LDU graph, never over
   `fvMesh`. Row offsets = `losortStartAddr()`, permutation = `losortAddr()`; build once per topology change,
   refill values every step. Store fp32, reduce fp64.
3. **Port `src/fused` rather than the unfused operators.** `fvcSurfaceOps`' in-place accumulation is a
   fused-multiply-add over a face list; use losort-segmented reduction instead of atomics.
4. **Bring up in tier order.** `scalarTransportFoam` and `surfactantFoam` first (one field, one matrix, fixed
   sparsity — a genuinely zero-round-trip loop). Then `acousticFoam` / `thermoFoam` /
   `solidEquilibriumDisplacementFoam`, where the matrix is assembled once so a single cuDSS factorisation serves
   the whole run. Then `dnsFoam` (the one place cuFFT is the whole point). Then the SRF and Boussinesq pairs
   (elementwise additions to an existing PIMPLE). Then the VOF group, once a device MULES limiter exists. Defer
   everything Lagrangian, overset-with-motion, isoAdvector and the adjoint outer loop.
5. **Handle `wallDist` explicitly.** It is the one tier-D dependency that gates SpalartAllmaras*, kOmegaSST*,
   EBRSM, SLADelta, IDDESDelta, vanDriestDelta and DEShybrid. Static mesh: compute on host once, upload once, done.
   Moving or topo-changing mesh: it must be solved on device or it reintroduces a per-step round-trip.
6. **Make `surfaceInterpolationScheme` GPU-aware, not just the models.** DEShybrid hands fresh face weights to the
   momentum `div` assembler every step; if the scheme registry cannot return a device-side weight array, the whole
   `div(phi,U)` assembly round-trips to the host.
7. **Resolve every runtime selection once, at setup.** Thermo, viscosity, turbulence, radiation, blending,
   interpolation — all E-cpu-only in both trees. Bake each into a fixed kernel via template or `if constexpr`
   specialisation. A virtual call per cell inside the loop makes the no-host-round-trip requirement unreachable.
